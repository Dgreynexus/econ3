FACTOR 01 7.8% (Oct 2026)

Inflation (Implasyon)

Up from 2.0% in Jan & 7.2% in Sept 2026

Business Operations
  • Higher cost of raw materials, fuel, and transport logistics.
  • Strong pressure to raise selling prices alongside smaller profit margins.
  • Lower purchasing power of customers reducing basket sizes.
Industry Development
  • Higher input and borrowing costs slow capital investment and expansion.
  • Fixed-price sectors and small MSMEs with low cash reserves are hit hardest.
2026 Philippine Context (PSA Data)

Headline inflation accelerated to 7.8% in October 2026 (from 2.0% in January and 7.2% in September 2026); rice inflation surged to 20.7%, while transport inflation hit 20.1% due to elevated fuel prices and public transport fares.

FACTOR 02 5.4% (Sept 2026)

Unemployment (Kawalan ng Trabaho)

2.86M jobless Filipinos (~11% underemployment)

Business Operations
  • Fewer customers with steady disposable spending power.
  • Larger available labor pool (easier hiring, but weaker overall consumer demand).
  • More frequent customer requests for informal credit (utang / lista).
Industry Development
  • Weak household demand slows growth across retail, food service, and personal services.
  • Possible rise in small informal businesses and home-based micro-ventures as displaced workers seek livelihood.
2026 Philippine Context (PSA Data)

Philippine unemployment rose to 5.4% in September 2026 (roughly 2.86 million jobless Filipinos, up from 3.8% in September 2025), while underemployment stood at around 11%.

FACTOR 03 2.1% Q3 2026 GDP

Income (Kita)

Down from 2.4% in Q2 2026 (Jan–Sept avg: 2.5%)

Business Operations
  • Lower real household income translates directly into lower unit sales volume.
  • Customer shift toward smaller, cheaper pack sizes.
  • Heightened pressure on business owners regarding minimum wages and worker welfare.
Industry Development
  • Slower macroeconomic growth reduces aggregate industry earnings and delays expansion plans.
2026 Philippine Context (PSA Data)

Q3 2026 Philippine GDP growth slowed to 2.1% (down from 2.4% in Q2 2026—the weakest pace since the pandemic), bringing the January–September 2026 average growth to 2.5%.

FACTOR 04 E-Wallets & Social Commerce

Technology (Teknolohiya)

GCash, Maya, QRPh & Delivery Platforms

Business Operations
  • E-wallets (GCash/Maya), online selling, and delivery apps open new markets and reduce physical overhead costs.
  • Requires upfront capital investment, digital literacy skills, and cybersecurity/scam protection.
Industry Development
  • Drives business model innovation, new digital industries, and tech-enabled jobs.
  • Widens the competitive gap between digitally adopting firms and traditional non-adopters.
2026 Philippine Context (PSA Data)

Micro and small Filipino sellers leverage social media, QRPh/e-wallets, and delivery platforms to reach buyers directly when physical walk-in foot traffic is slow.

FACTOR 05 Tingi & Value Trading

Consumer Behavior (Gawi ng Mamimili)

Sachet portions & generic/value brand switching

Business Operations
  • Shoppers trade down to cheaper or generic brands, buy tingi (micro-portions), and compare prices online.
  • Businesses actively adjust product SKUs, downsize pack formats, and run targeted value promos.
Industry Development
  • Shifting consumer demand reshapes which product categories and industries expand or contract.
2026 Philippine Context (PSA Data)

Filipino households stretch tight daily budgets by buying cooking oil, condiments, and essentials in smaller tingi portions and switching to lower-priced alternatives amid elevated food and transport costs.
